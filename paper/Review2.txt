In this paper, theory is developed that enables the counting, in reasonable computational time, of the numbers of finite semirings both up to isomorphism, and up to isomorphism/anti-isomorphism, for small values of n. The theory developed, while short, is elegant and insightful, and the tables of results provided are helpful. I think that this paper provides a meaningful contribution to semigroup theory, and should be published following revisions, detailed below:

---How is distribution being checked? Theorem 1.3 says that IF \sigma and \tau are permutations that give semirings, then the semirings they give are isomorphic iff the permutations belong to the same double coset. Not all permutations give rise to semirings, though, so computing representatives of double cosets therefore gives an upper bound on the number of semirings. How are you checking/handling which permutations give semirings? Are you computing all double cosets and then checking one representative per coset for distributivity? If so, please mention this and also mention that distributivity is constant on cosets. If you are not computing all double cosets, then how are you filtering prior to computation?

> This is an excellent point, we have reformulated Theorem 1.3, and added a new corollary that explicitly spells this out. The previous version of Theorem 1.3 assumed that (S, +, x^\sigma) and (S, + , x^\tau) were semirings, but didn't use this in the proof at all. We've removed this assumption.
